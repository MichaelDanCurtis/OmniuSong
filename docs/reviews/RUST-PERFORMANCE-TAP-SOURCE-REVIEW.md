## Findings and disposition

One concrete race was found and corrected: off-callback `TapControl::cancel` and receiver abandonment independently sampled the last completed callback index and frame count. Concurrent End publication could combine an older index with a newer frame. `completed_prefix` now makes a bounded coherent read under the version counter; its Busy fallback deliberately omits a correlated callback index. The sampled frame is documented as an off-callback observation, not an exact failure boundary. The owner regression fixes an odd publication version deterministically and checks this distinction for both cancellation and abandonment. No additional blocking defect was identified in the reviewed public path.

The following properties are supported by the source audit:

- **Reservation precedes musical mutation.** `live.rs::render` validates the whole frame extent and calls `begin_callback` before `boundary`. The tap reserves `2 + 4 + ceil(frames / 256)` slots, at most 70, in a fixed 256-slot ring. Single-producer ownership makes the checked available capacity a lower bound until that callback finishes. Reservation failure clears/silences without applying or consuming new batches. Output-buffer frames still advance, while the completed recording prefix remains unchanged; those are intentionally separate counters.
- **Original receipt and application remain distinct.** `boundary` has one four-packet budget per native invocation, including empty, stale, Panic-discarded and fault-rejected packets. `apply` validates shape before indexing and stages the entire source/voice transaction. The tap retains the full fixed batch with its disposition; malformed rejected originals are copied without indexing their invalid length or pretending they are valid GUI-source events. Public stale admission creates no fictitious callback receipt.
- **The audio is the actual logical bus.** Each PCM packet copies the same finite post-mix F32 value used by the renderer before F32/I16/U16 device conversion. The first tap has no GUI clock descriptor, host-time conversion or calibration; observed GUI ticks remain exact opaque values bound to the live scope. Stored F32 is not represented as native integer quantization or physical sound.
- **Callback work is explicitly bounded.** The accepted invocation is at most 16,384 frames × eight channels, with 64 voices including release tails, four ingress packets, 64 PCM packets, two aggregate clear slots and 128 automatic-transition slots. The transition bound follows from at most one automatic release and one end for each resident voice; voices are not allocated during the sample loop. Fixed arrays and enum errors are used throughout the callback path. No callback path acquires the receiver mutex, serializes JSON, accesses files, sleeps or waits. Allocation counters verify heap behavior; those counters alone do not prove absence of lock or I/O calls, which was checked in source.
- **Exact-end lease evidence is retained.** After each rendered frame, `audible_at(self.frame)` calls `clear_for_fault` on expiry, including expiry at the exclusive end of a callback. The clear is recorded at that exact frame, rather than moved to the next callback. Pulse/patch automatic release and envelope end are separate engine transitions. They do not become invented human NoteOffs. Source-owned sustain and original 16-bit release values remain intact.
- **Pending fault publication is truthful.** The existing first-fault WRITING sentinel can require immediate silence before the cause is readable. `clear_for_fault` emits `FaultPending` once at the actual clear frame, without spinning or guessing. A later End may expose the resolved first fault; resolving it does not duplicate or retime the clear. The new private owner test holds this publication window deterministically and then resolves it. Another private test injects malformed fixed input behind admission and verifies rejection before indexing.
- **A partial callback is not a completed prefix.** Begin/ordinal/PCM coverage/End form a checked group. A mid-callback receiver cancellation or transfer failure can occur after earlier output frames were supplied; the remaining buffer is silenced and no End is manufactured. The first cause stays sticky, queued originals remain drainable, and draining does not resume a faulted recording. A complete End means only a complete in-memory callback group; it is neither a durable checkpoint nor a normally sealed performance.
- **Recording backing survives callback teardown.** `LiveRenderer::drop` explicitly drops the entire recording wrapper, including the producer's Arc fields, before publishing `render_done`. The producer acknowledgment follows rtrb producer destruction. The private admission retention guard holds the consumer/backing off callback; premature loss retains one bounded allocation for process retirement rather than making callback destruction free the ring. The sole receiver is not clonable. Integrators must retain admission through actual renderer and error-closure destruction: `TapSnapshot.producer_destroyed` alone is not that complete lifecycle proof.

The constructor and destructor remain subject to that ownership contract. The new library constructor is not a substitute for the existing native stop/pause/closure protocol. No process or device quiescence is proved merely by constructing and dropping a `LiveErrorGuard` in a unit test.

The first Clippy pass rejected the deliberately large packet enum. The coordinator added one local `large_enum_variant` allowance with its fixed-`Copy`/no-heap rationale; boxing PCM or End would introduce callback allocation/ownership. Both variants remain covered by compile-time packet (`<= 16 KiB`) and ring (`<= 4 MiB`) assertions. This is a narrow representation choice, not a broad warning suppression or a performance measurement. The final all-target/all-feature Clippy run passed with warnings denied.
